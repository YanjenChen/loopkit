# The monitor

The monitor is a Claude artifact page that displays a run's ledger. It makes no decisions of
its own: every verdict, front and best value it shows comes from the framework.

## Blocks

1. **Status**: run, batch progress and stop conditions, counts (kept, reverted, failed,
   human, front size, queued requests), the latest ITER or HUMAN line, the time of the last
   update, the stop reason, and pattern highlights: kept rate per proposer, the most changed
   files, the last iterations.
2. **One objective**: the objective by iteration. Kept and reverted candidates are dots,
   failed ones a cross on the axis, the best value so far a dashed step line, new batches a
   vertical divider. Human candidates are diamonds at the iteration when they were scored,
   hollow until promoted.
3. **Several objectives**: the Pareto front. Front members are highlighted and joined by a
   staircase, other candidates are grey, c000 and the latest candidate are labeled, and an
   arrow points to the better corner. With more than two objectives, choose the axes. The
   timeline slider shows the front as of any record, Play animates it, and the fronts at
   earlier batch starts are drawn faded.
4. **Evolve tree**: one row per candidate, top to bottom in recording order; edges from
   parents to children, dashed for the second parent of a merge; human candidates are dashed
   diamonds; front members are ringed. Failed candidates can be hidden.
5. **Candidates**: newest first, collapsed to the ITER or HUMAN line; expanded, the idea,
   proposer, learned note, objectives against the parent (or c000 for human candidates),
   constraints, extra values, reason, files, parents, commit and ref, and times.

Clicking a point or a tree row opens that candidate in the list.

## Data

The page is generated by `loopkit monitor html` from
`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/monitor/monitor.html`. It reads the artifact's database:

| Document | Content |
|---|---|
| `meta/current` | the run name, objectives (order, direction, unit, tolerance), constraint and extra names, status (batch, counts, front, queue), pattern analysis, `latest_seq` |
| `records/chunk-NNNNN` | the ledger records `25*N+1` to `25*N+25`, each with `derived`: the front after it, the best value so far (one objective), its ITER or HUMAN line, its iteration within the batch |

Records are grouped in chunks because an artifact's database holds at most 5,000 documents.
A record's derived fields depend only on the records before it, so rewriting a chunk is
harmless. The monitor gets a display copy of each record: long texts are shortened, and
`extra` is cut to its short scalar values (marked `extra_truncated`) when it is large, so a
chunk stays under the database's 256 KiB document limit. The ledger keeps everything.

The page is published with `capabilities: {"db": {"rules": [{"path": "", "read": "view",
"write": "admin"}]}}`: viewers read, only editors (the owner) write. A page with a
database is organization-internal.

## Pushing

`loopkit monitor push` writes the chunks that changed since the last acknowledged push, plus
`meta/current`, as JSON files under the run's `work/monitor/`, and prints the `write_db`
batch to send with the Artifact tool. `loopkit monitor ack <seq>` records what was pushed.
A batch holds at most 49 chunks; when more are waiting, the command says so, and another
push after the ack sends the rest. A push that fails is simply repeated at the next
iteration. In the run's agent worktree the Artifact tool is allowed by the run's permission
settings, so an unattended run does not stop at a prompt.

Until the first push, the page shows the sample data embedded by `monitor html --sample`,
under a banner saying so.
